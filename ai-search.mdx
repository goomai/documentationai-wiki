## What AI Search is

AI Search is built for research questions, not only keywords. Ask a question and you get:

1. A **synthesized answer** with in-text citations
2. The **papers** the answer draws on, so you can check every claim
3. **Related questions** to keep exploring

AI Search looks in research papers (200M+ public papers), PubMed, arXiv, and your Reference manager. It doesn't search the web, blogs, or general internet content.

If you'd rather write an exact search string, switch to **Keyword** search. See [Keyword search](#keyword-search) below.

## When to use it

Use AI Search when you want:

- A grounded overview of what the literature says on a question
- A strong starting set of papers without manual filtering
- A quick evidence check before a fuller review

Use a different tool when:

| You want to | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Search, screen, extract, and draft in one session | [Research Agent](/research-agent) |
| Get a cited literature review with a comparison table | [Literature Review](/literature-review-agent) |
| Get a long, detailed report on a topic | [Deep Research Report](/deep-research-report) |
| Ask questions about PDFs you already have | [Chat with PDF](/chat-with-pdf) |

## Run a search

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open AI Search" icon="search" title-type="p">

  On your Dashboard or in **New Chat**, select the **Search** tab. You can also open **More tools** → **AI Search** in the sidebar.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask your question" icon="edit" title-type="p">

  Make sure **AI Search** is selected in the **AI Search | Keyword** switch. Then type a question, topic, paper title, or plain-language description of what you want to find out.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose sources and filters (optional)" icon="sliders-horizontal" title-type="p">

  All sources are on by default. Click the sources button to turn **Research papers**, **PubMed**, **arXiv**, or **Reference manager** on or off. At least one source must stay on.

  When you search research papers, click **Filters** to narrow the results. See [Filters](#filters).

  </Step>

  <Step title="Read the answer" icon="file-text" title-type="p">

  Send your question. The answer opens in a chat, with the papers behind it. Ask follow-up questions in the same chat, or pick a related question.

  </Step>
</Steps>

### Ask good questions

AI Search works best with clear, focused questions, such as:

- Does regular physical exercise reduce the risk of chronic diseases?
- Does gut microbiome diversity affect immune system function?
- What are the pros and cons of immunotherapy in treating autoimmune diseases?

## Work with the results

- **Hover a citation** to see the paper's details. Click **Click to see supporting quotes** to see the sentences behind the claim.
- **Open the papers list** to see every paper the answer cites, with its journal, year, and citation count.
- **Save papers**: click **My References** under a paper to add it to your Reference Manager. To save several, select them and click **Add to My References**, or use **Save References** to save all of them to a folder.

## Filters

Filters narrow the papers searched. You can filter by:

- **Open Access**: free to read, no paywall
- **Has PDF**: full text available
- **Publication year**: all, last 2 years, last 5 years, or a custom range
- **Min citations**
- **Journal quality**: journal quartile (Q1 to Q4), SNIP score, and SJR score
- **Publication details**: language and paper type, such as article, review, or preprint
- **Fields of study**

Click **Apply filters** to use them, or **Reset filters** to clear them.

<Callout kind="info">

Once you set a SNIP or SJR score range, papers without a journal score are left out.

</Callout>

## Keyword search

Keyword search finds papers that match an exact Boolean search string, like a PubMed search. Use it when you need a precise, repeatable search.

1. On the **Search** tab, switch to **Keyword**.
2. Type your search string. Type **/** to insert operators and fields, or click **Guide** for the full syntax.
3. Optionally, choose sources (**Research papers**, **PubMed**, **arXiv**) and click **Filters**.
4. Click **Search**.

Example: `(adolescent* OR teenager*) AND "sleep deprivation"[ti] AND 2015:2024[dp]`

| Write it as | Matches |
| --- | --- |
| `sleep AND memory` | Papers with both words |
| `teenager OR adolescent` | Papers with either word |
| `diabetes NOT gestational` | Papers with the first word and without the second |
| `(teenager OR adolescent) AND sleep` | Groups terms |
| `"sleep deprivation"` | The exact phrase |
| `random*` | Word endings, such as randomized or randomly |
| `sleep[ti]`, `sleep[ab]` | The word in the title or abstract |
| `2015:2024[dp]` | A year or range of years |
| `english[la]` | Papers in that language |

Write AND, OR, and NOT in capitals. Operators are read left to right, so use brackets to group terms.

In the results, sort by **Relevance**, **Newest**, or **Most cited**. Select papers to **Add to My References**, or click **Export** to download them as BibTeX, CSV, or Excel.
