## What is the Chrome Extension for PDFs?

The Paperguide Chrome extension helps you retrieve PDFs for the references in your library. When you have metadata-only references imported through DOI, BibTeX, or RIS without the actual PDF, the extension can find and attach the PDF from publisher websites, repositories, and databases.

You can also use the extension while you browse. Open a paper page on a publisher site or preprint server, then use the extension to download and attach the PDF to your library in one click.

## Installing the extension

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install from the Chrome Web Store" icon="download" title-type="p">

  Search for the Paperguide extension in the Chrome Web Store, then click **Add to Chrome**.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Pin the extension" icon="plus" title-type="p">

  Pin the extension to your toolbar so you can access it quickly while browsing research sites.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Log in" icon="users" title-type="p">

  Click the extension icon and log in with your Paperguide account. The extension connects to your current workspace.

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="tip">

Pinning the extension makes PDF retrieval faster when you move between publisher pages, repositories, and your library.

</Callout>

## Getting PDFs for existing references

Use this flow when a reference is already in your library but does not have an attached PDF.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a reference" icon="book-open" title-type="p">

  Open the reference in Reference Manager that needs a PDF.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Get PDF" icon="download" title-type="p">

  Click the **Get PDF** button on the reference. The extension attempts to find an open-access version of the paper.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the PDF was attached" icon="check-circle" title-type="p">

  If the extension finds a PDF, it attaches the file to the reference and queues it for indexing.

  A successful result shows the PDF on the reference and updates the indexing status shortly after.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Getting PDFs from publisher pages

Use the extension directly from a paper page when you find an article outside your library.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Navigate to a paper page" icon="monitor" title-type="p">

  Open a paper page on a publisher website, preprint server such as arXiv or bioRxiv, or a repository such as PubMed Central or Semantic Scholar.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Click the smart action button" icon="zap" title-type="p">

  The extension detects research papers automatically and shows a smart action button. Click it to download and attach the PDF to your library.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Check where the PDF was saved" icon="check-circle" title-type="p">

  The PDF is attached to the matching reference in your library. If the paper is not already in your library, Paperguide creates a new reference for it.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## PDF retrieval sources

The extension looks for PDFs across several common sources:

- **Publisher websites** — direct download from the publisher page
- **Preprint servers** — arXiv, bioRxiv, and medRxiv
- **Repositories** — PubMed Central and institutional repositories
- **Open-access versions** — open-access discovery services and other OA finders

<Callout kind="alert">

PDF availability depends on access rights. Open-access papers work best. For paywalled papers, the extension can retrieve the PDF only when you have institutional access or an open-access version is available.

</Callout>

## Tracking indexing status

After Paperguide adds a PDF, it queues the file for indexing. The status appears on the reference so you can tell whether the paper is ready for AI features.

- **Pending** — the PDF was uploaded, but indexing has not started
- **Indexing** — Paperguide is processing the file for text extraction and vectorization
- **Indexed** — the PDF is fully processed and available for chat and search
- **Failed** — indexing did not complete, often because the PDF is corrupted, scanned without OCR, or unsupported

## What vectorization does

Indexed PDFs become searchable because Paperguide converts the document text into embeddings. That processing makes features such as [Chat with PDF](/chat-with-pdf) and [Research Agent](/research-agent) work against the paper's actual content.

## Retrieving PDFs in bulk

Select multiple references in your library to attempt batch PDF retrieval. Paperguide processes each reference in sequence and tries to find and attach a PDF for each one.

Bulk retrieval is useful after importing a large set of metadata-only references. You can fill in missing PDFs without opening each reference one by one.
