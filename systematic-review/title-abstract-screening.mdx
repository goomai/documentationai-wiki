## What is Title & Abstract Screening?

The first screening pass. Paperguide AI reads the title and abstract of every paper in your confirmed set, evaluates it against your criteria, and shows you the evidence behind every decision. A person then verifies that work before the review moves on.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  **Before you start:** Your abstract screening criteria must be defined in the [Protocol](/systematic-review/protocol), and your papers must be confirmed in [Papers](/systematic-review/papers). Assign your verifier before starting, not after.&#x20;
</Callout>

## How this stage works

1. **A Full Access member starts AI screening.** AI evaluates every paper against your research question and abstract criteria.
2. **Results appear as they are produced.** You can begin reviewing before the full run finishes.
3. **A verifier reviews the decisions.** They check each one against the abstract and the evidence AI cited, and override anything that is wrong.
4. **The verifier confirms the papers.** Only then does the next stage unlock.

Nothing advances on AI output alone. Verification is what moves the review forward.

## Before you start

### Set up your criteria

AI screens against the abstract screening criteria in your protocol. If they are not defined, this stage cannot run.

Criterion order matters. When a paper fails, the **first** criterion it fails, in the order you listed them, becomes its exclusion reason. Order your criteria so the exclusion reasons your review reports are the ones that actually matter.

[Set up your protocol](/systematic-review/protocol)

### Assign a verifier

Decide who is responsible for verifying this stage and assign them before screening starts. Assignment determines who can act on the results and who gets notified when the run completes.

| If the stage is        | Who verifies it                                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Assigned to a member   | That member. They receive the notification when screening finishes.                                   |
| Not assigned to anyone | Any Full Access or Review member. All of them are notified and can work through the results together. |

View members can never verify, assigned or not.

[Members, access & roles](/systematic-review/members-roles)

## Running AI screening

Onl&#x79;**&#x20;Full Access&#x20;**&#x6D;embers ca&#x6E;**&#x20;start AI screening.** Review members verify results but cannot trigger the run.

Screening a large set takes time, but you do not have to wait for it. Results appear as papers are processed, and a progress banner tracks the run, so the verifier can start working through decisions while the rest are still being evaluated. Paperguide also sends an email when the run completes, to the assigned verifier or, if the stage is unassigned, to every Full Access and Review member.

## How AI reaches a decision

AI evaluates each paper one criterion at a time, then derives a single overall decision from those results.

**For every criterion**, it returns a verdict and the evidence behind it:

| Verdict     | What it means                                          |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Meets**   | The abstract satisfies this criterion                  |
| **Fails**   | The abstract does not satisfy this criterion           |
| **Unclear** | The abstract does not give enough information to judge |

Each verdict comes with cited evidence: the specific text from the abstract that AI based it on. This is what makes a decision checkable rather than something you either trust or do not.

**The overall decision** follows from those verdicts:

| If                                                  | Overall decision                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any criterion **fails**                             | **Exclude**, with that criterion as the exclusion reason |
| No criterion fails, but at least one is **unclear** | **Maybe**                                                |
| Every criterion **meets**                           | **Include**                                              |

Read the criterion verdicts before changing an overall decision. When you disagree with a result, the cause is almost always one criterion that was judged differently than you would have judged it, and that is the level to fix it at.

This logic keeps borderline papers in the review rather than dropping them early. A paper you cannot judge from its abstract becomes a **Maybe** and moves forward to be settled on its full text. In a systematic review, losing a relevant study is a more serious error than carrying an irrelevant one into the next stage.

## Working through the results

Screening opens in a table view: papers on the left, abstract details on the right. Select a paper to open its detailed view, with the full abstract, the current decision, every criterion verdict, and the cited evidence.

Papers are grouped into four tabs so you can review by outcome: **All**, **Included**, **Excluded**, and **Maybe**. The Maybe tab is the one worth your attention first, since it holds the papers where AI could not reach a clean judgment and human reading matters most.

To manage a large queue:

- **Filter by decision** to work through one outcome at a time
- **Filter by verification status** to find papers that still need confirming
- **Sort** by title, year, citations, or decision date

Sorting by decision date and filtering to unconfirmed papers gives you a clean queue during an active run, with newly screened papers arriving at the end.

## Verifying and overriding

The AI decision is a recommendation. A confirmed decision is one a person has reviewed and accepted.

**To verify a paper**, open it and check the criterion verdicts against the abstract, paying most attention to anything marked fails or unclear, since those drive the outcome. If you agree, confirm it. If you disagree, override the decision to Include, Maybe, or Exclude. When you override to Exclude, select which criterion the paper failed.

If the abstract genuinely does not support a clean decision, leave the paper as **Maybe**. That is the correct answer, not an avoided one.

**To confirm**, either confirm papers individually as you review them, or select **Confirm All** once you have worked through the set. Confirming records the AI decision as approved, or your override as the final decision. Both are kept, so the review holds a record of what AI decided and what a person changed.

Full Access members can review and override decisions after verification is complete, including decisions made by another member. This gives a review lead a way to correct the record without reopening the stage for everyone.

## Confirming the stage

Once every paper is confirmed, you see the final counts: **Included**, **Maybe**, and **Excluded**. These feed the screening block of your PRISMA diagram.

Included and Maybe papers both move forward. Excluded papers stop here.

Confirming unlocks the next stage. Depending on how you set up the review, that is either [Full Text Screening](/systematic-review/full-text-screening) or, if you disabled full text screening, [Data Extraction](/systematic-review/data-extraction).

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  The next stage stays locked until this one is confirmed. If screening has finished but nothing is moving, check whether the papers still need confirming.
</Callout>

## If your protocol or papers change

Screening can run again when the review changes underneath it.

**If you change your abstract criteria** in the protocol, screening may need to run again so decisions stay consistent with the criteria you are now using. How much needs rescreening depends on what changed. This is why the protocol is worth settling before you start.

**If you add papers** to the collection, only the new papers need screening. Work already confirmed is not affected, and a verifier reviews the new results the same way.

[Adding papers after screening has started](/systematic-review/papers#adding-papers-after-screening-has-started)

You can export your abstract screening results, including decisions, exclusion reasons, and the evidence behind them. Useful for sharing progress with collaborators outside Paperguide and for keeping a screening record alongside your submission.
