## What is the Protocol?

The protocol is the blueprint for your systematic review. It defines your research question, screening criteria for each stage, and extraction fields that determine what data the AI pulls from included papers.

You set up the protocol before screening begins. The AI then uses that protocol to apply the same rules across all papers, which keeps screening and extraction consistent and reproducible.

The AI generates the protocol from your research question and any additional context you provided. You can confirm it as-is, edit any criterion or field, or build the protocol from scratch. Save only when the protocol reflects your actual review design.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  You stay in control of the protocol. AI suggestions are never saved automatically — you decide what to keep.
</Callout>

## AI-generated protocol

When you open the Protocol stage for the first time, the system generates the protocol from your research question and additional context. It creates separate outputs for each enabled stage, so you only see **Title & Abstract Screeing** or **Full-text Screening** criteria if those stages are part of your review.

The generated protocol includes:

- **Abstract screening criteria** for title & abstract screening, if enabled
- **Full-text screening criteria** for full-text screening, if enabled
- **Extraction fields** for data extraction

The AI generates the protocol as a methodologist-oriented assistant using established systematic review frameworks. Depending on your question type, it structures suggestions around frameworks such as PICO, PEO, CoCoPop, or SPIDER.

Stage-specific guidance matters. Title and Abstract criteria must be answerable from the title and abstract alone, while Full Text criteria can rely on details available only in the paper itself.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the protocol" icon="check-circle" title-type="p">
    Read each suggested criterion and extraction field against your actual review question.

    Check that the titles are specific, the instructions are clear, and the pass or fail conditions reflect your intended eligibility rules.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Edit what the AI suggested" icon="edit" title-type="p">
    Update any criterion or field that is too broad, too narrow, or ambiguous.

    Add your own custom criteria and fields where the generated protocol misses important study characteristics, populations, interventions, outcomes, or context.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save the protocol" icon="save" title-type="p">
    Save criteria and extraction fields only after they reflect the rules you want the AI to apply.

    Nothing is persisted until you save, so you can revise the protocol freely before committing it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Screening criteria

Screening criteria are the inclusion and exclusion rules the AI applies to each paper. Each criterion is evaluated independently, and all criteria carry equal weight.

Each screening criterion includes the same four parts:

<ParamField body="title" param-type="string" required="true" deprecated="false" show-location="false">
  A short label for the criterion. Suggested titles are typically 2 to 5 words so you can scan them quickly during review.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="instructions" param-type="string" required="false" deprecated="false" show-location="false">
  Guidance that tells the AI how to interpret the criterion. Use this field to clarify edge cases, terminology, or how strictly the rule should be applied.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="includeWhen" param-type="string" required="false" deprecated="false" show-location="false">
  The concrete condition that means a paper passes the criterion. Write this as an observable rule, not as a general goal.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="excludeWhen" param-type="string" required="false" deprecated="false" show-location="false">
  The concrete condition that means a paper fails the criterion. Clear exclusion rules reduce inconsistent decisions across papers.
</ParamField>

### How criteria differ by stage

Title and Abstract screening is designed to be sensitive rather than final. If the abstract is silent or ambiguous for a criterion, the AI keeps the paper in play instead of excluding it.

Full Text screening is confirmatory. At that stage, the AI uses information from the full paper to make the final eligibility decision.

#### Title and Abstract

Use this stage to filter obvious mismatches without excluding papers that may still be relevant.

The AI follows this decision pattern:

- If any criterion is clearly a **No**, the paper is excluded.
- If every criterion is **Yes**, the paper is included.
- If at least one criterion is **Maybe** and none are **No**, the paper moves forward for further review.

This makes the stage conservative by design. Ambiguity leads to review, not exclusion.

#### Full Text

Use this stage to confirm final eligibility from the full paper.

Full Text screening is stricter:

- If every criterion is **Yes**, the paper is included.
- If any criterion fails, the paper is excluded.

At this stage, the goal is final inclusion or exclusion rather than broad recall.

### Exclusion reasons

Every excluded paper gets a reason. The reason is the first failing criterion — the first criterion evaluated as a clear **No** among the saved criteria for that stage.

Because the exclusion reason is the first failing criterion in order, the position of each criterion matters. If two criteria both fail for a paper, the one listed first becomes the stated reason for exclusion. This is why reordering criteria can change which criterion is cited as the exclusion reason, even though the paper is still excluded either way.

### Reordering criteria

Drag criteria to reorder them within a stage. Reordering does not change how each criterion is evaluated or trigger re-screening — a position change by itself is not treated as a substantive criteria edit.

However, reordering does affect exclusion reasons. Because the exclusion reason is the first failing criterion in order, moving a criterion earlier or later can change which criterion is cited as the reason for a paper's exclusion. Keep this in mind when reorganizing, especially if certain criteria are more meaningful as primary exclusion reasons in your review.

### Previewing changes

If screening has already run, preview criteria changes before you save them. The preview compares your proposed criteria to the last frozen protocol snapshot for that stage, not only to the current on-screen list.

The preview shows:

- **Added criteria**
- **Deleted criteria**
- **Updated criteria**
- **How many papers need re-screening**
- **Cascade effects** into later stages

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
  A content change to Title and Abstract criteria can invalidate later Full Text and extraction results. A content change to Full Text criteria can invalidate extraction results.
</Callout>

Pure reordering does not trigger re-screening. Changes to the criterion content do.

## Extraction fields

Extraction fields define what data the AI pulls from papers that pass Full Text screening. The generated protocol usually includes a mix of standard study descriptors and fields specific to your research question.

Every extraction field contains the following parts:

<ParamField body="title" param-type="string" required="true" deprecated="false" show-location="false">
  A short field name such as `Study design`, `Sample size`, or `Setting`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="instructions" param-type="string" required="false" deprecated="false" show-location="false">
  Add instructions to guide on what to extract, which details to capture, and how to present the answer.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="outputType" param-type="string" required="true" deprecated="false" show-location="false" enum="answer,yes_maybe_no,specified">
  Controls the response format the AI must return for the field.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="options" param-type="array" required="false" deprecated="false" show-location="false">
  A list of allowed options used only when `outputType` is `specified`. Each option can include a label and an optional definition.
</ParamField>

### Output types

Use the output type that best matches the data you need. More structure usually improves consistency, but only if the options map cleanly to the evidence in the paper.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="answer" icon="edit">
    Use `answer` for open-ended values such as sample size, country, or intervention description.

    This type works best when the paper may phrase the answer in many valid ways or when you need a direct extracted value.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="yes_maybe_no" icon="help-circle">
    Use `yes_maybe_no` when the field represents a categorical judgment rather than a free-text fact.

    This type is useful when the paper may support a clear yes, a clear no, or an uncertain answer.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="specified" icon="check-circle">
    Use `specified` when the answer should come from a fixed list you define.

    Add option definitions if two labels could be confused. Better option definitions usually produce cleaner extraction results.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Building better extraction fields

Write field titles and instructions so a reviewer could apply them without extra explanation. If a field asks for a judgment, define what counts as evidence. If a field asks for a value, specify the format you want returned.

For `specified` fields, keep the option list complete and mutually distinct. Overlapping options make extraction less reliable and harder to interpret later.

## Narrative sections

Your protocol does more than guide screening. It also shapes parts of the final report.

The Methods section is built directly from protocol data and review activity. It uses your screening criteria, extraction fields, search information, and PRISMA flow counts to describe what you actually did.

Other report sections are written later from the extracted evidence. Findings, synthesis, and conclusion sections are AI-written, while the Methods section is generated deterministically from the saved protocol and review outputs.

## Protocol snapshots

Each screening run freezes the current protocol into a snapshot before the run starts. That snapshot preserves the exact criteria used for that run, so later edits do not rewrite the basis of past decisions.

Snapshots are append-only and versioned by stage. Every screening or extraction result can be traced back to the snapshot that produced it, which supports reproducibility and auditability.

<Callout kind="alert" collapsed="false">
  You cannot edit screening criteria while a screening run is active for that stage. Cancel the active run first, then make changes and start a new run.
</Callout>

### Why snapshots matter

Without snapshots, changing a criterion after screening would make old results hard to interpret. A paper could appear to have been screened under rules that did not exist when the decision was made.

Snapshots prevent that problem. They preserve the protocol as it existed at run time and make change impact visible when you revise criteria later.
