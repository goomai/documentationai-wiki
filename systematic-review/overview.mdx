## What is Systematic Review?

Systematic Review is an AI-assisted platform for conducting rigorous systematic reviews following PRISMA guidelines. It guides you through a multi-stage pipeline from importing papers to generating a final report with a PRISMA flow diagram, with AI handling the heavy lifting at each stage and humans verifying decisions.

The pipeline supports both AI-led mode, where AI screens and you verify, and dual-reviewer mode, where two independent reviewers work through decisions with conflict resolution for teams that need to meet methodological requirements.

## The review pipeline

A systematic review moves through four stages in order.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Papers" icon="book-open" title-type="p">
    Import and manage your paper pool. Add papers through AI Search, PDF upload, URL or DOI, BibTeX or RIS, or directly from your Reference Manager. Automatic duplicate detection helps keep the pool clean.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Protocol" icon="settings" title-type="p">
    Define your research question, screening criteria, and extraction fields. The AI generates an initial protocol from your research question, which you review and edit before screening starts.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Title & Abstract Screening" icon="check-circle" title-type="p">
    The first screening stage evaluates each paper by title and abstract against your criteria. The AI produces a decision of Include, Exclude, or Maybe with criterion-level verdicts and cited evidence from the abstract. You then verify or override each decision.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Full Text Screening" icon="search" title-type="p">
    Papers included from the previous stage move into full-text screening. The AI reads each PDF and evaluates every criterion with evidence cited directly from the document, so you can review the decision alongside the source text.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Data Extraction" icon="database" title-type="p">
    Papers that pass full-text screening move into extraction. The AI fills the structured fields defined in your protocol, and results appear in a grid with papers as rows and fields as columns for review, editing, and export.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Report" icon="file-text" title-type="p">
    The final stage generates a narrative report with inline citations and a PRISMA flow diagram showing paper counts across the review. Export the report when you are ready to share findings or prepare for publication.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Systematic Review runs screening in sequence: title and abstract screening first, then full-text screening, then extraction, and finally report generation.
</Callout>

## PRISMA diagram

The PRISMA flow diagram is computed live from your review's current state, so it updates automatically as papers move through the pipeline. You do not need to regenerate it manually to see current counts.

The diagram shows four parts of the review flow:

- **Identification**: papers from database searches, imported papers, duplicates removed, and papers removed for other reasons
- **Screening**: papers screened and excluded at the title and abstract stage, with exclusion reason counts
- **Eligibility**: papers sought for full-text retrieval, papers not retrieved because PDFs are missing, and papers assessed and excluded at the full-text stage
- **Included**: the final count of papers included in the review

The PRISMA view is available to all review members at any point in the pipeline.

## Team collaboration

Systematic Review supports team-based collaboration with role-based access control. Each review member has one of three roles: **Full Access**, **Reviewer**, or **View**.

|                                | **Full Access** | **Reviewer**    | **View** |
| ------------------------------ | --------------- | --------------- | -------- |
| **View all stages**            | Yes             | Yes             | Yes      |
| **Add papers**                 | Yes             | Yes             | No       |
| **Initiate AI tasks/runs**     | Yes             | No              | No       |
| **Change screening decisions** | Yes             | With assignment | No       |
| **Confirm AI decisions**       | Yes             | With assignment | No       |
| **Edit protocol**              | Yes             | No              | No       |
| **Edit settings**              | Yes             | No              | No       |
| **Manage team roles**          | Yes             | No              | No       |
| **Manage members**             | Creator         | No              | No       |
| **Export**                     | Yes             | Yes             | No       |

Review members with the **Reviewer** role can be assigned to specific stage responsibilities. In AI-led mode, that usually means verifying AI decisions. In dual-reviewer mode, assigned reviewers submit independent decisions and a conflict resolver settles disagreements when reviewers do not agree.

## Managing members and roles

Only review managers can invite members, change roles, and manage access for a systematic review. Review managers include the review creator, workspace owners, and organization owners or admins. Regular workspace editors do not get manager privileges, even if they have full access to the review itself.

### How members get access

People can join a review in three ways:

- **Workspace membership**: Members of the team workspace automatically get access based on their workspace role. Workspace editors default to the Reviewer role, workspace viewers default to View, and workspace owners get Full Access with manager privileges.
- **Email invitation**: Invite anyone by email, including people outside your workspace, and assign them a specific review role: Full Access, Reviewer, or View. They receive an email invitation and must accept before they can access the review.
- **Share link**: Generate a view-only link that anyone with the link can use to join the review. People who redeem the link appear as guests in the member list.

### Inviting members by email

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the review's member management panel" icon="settings" title-type="p">
    Go to your systematic review and open the members or team settings.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Invite by email" icon="mail" title-type="p">
    Click the invite option, enter the email address, and choose a role: Full Access, Reviewer, or View.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Recipient accepts" icon="check" title-type="p">
    The invitee receives an email invitation. Once they accept, they appear in the member list with the role you assigned.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Changing a member's role

Managers can override a workspace member's review role to give them more or fewer permissions than their workspace default. For example, you can promote a workspace viewer to Reviewer for a specific review, or restrict an editor to View.

Clearing the override reverts the member to their workspace default role. When a member is changed to View, any stage assignments they had are automatically reset.

### Assigning reviewers to stages

In both screening modes, you can assign specific people to stage roles. In AI-led mode, assign reviewers as verifiers to confirm AI decisions. In dual-reviewer mode, assign two independent reviewers and a conflict resolver for each screening stage.

Assignments are managed in the review's settings, and reviewers must have Reviewer or Full Access to be assigned. When a reviewer is assigned as a verifier, they receive an email notification.

### Removing members

To remove an invited guest, revoke their access from the member management panel. They lose access immediately. For workspace members, clearing a role override reverts them to their workspace default.

To fully remove a workspace member from the review, remove them from the workspace itself.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Managers can also revoke a share link at any time. People who already joined through the link keep their access until you revoke them individually.
</Callout>

## Creating a review

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your research question" icon="help-circle" title-type="p">
    Start with your research question. You can use the AI question enhancer to diagnose and improve the question before you create the review, and those question enhancement actions do not consume credits.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure screening stages" icon="settings" title-type="p">
    Title and abstract screening and full-text screening are both enabled by default. Turn either stage off during setup if your workflow does not require it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add optional context" icon="edit" title-type="p">
    Add additional context if the research question alone is not enough to guide protocol generation. This field is optional and helps the AI produce more relevant screening criteria and extraction fields.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create the review" icon="check" title-type="p">
    Create the review when the setup looks right. The system generates the review title from your research question, creates default stage assignments, and takes you to the next part of the workflow.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  The screening mode is permanent after creation. If your methodology requires independent reviewer agreement, choose dual-reviewer mode before you create the review.
</Callout>

## Review overview page

After you create a review, the overview page shows progress across the pipeline. Each stage card reflects the current status, such as not started, in progress, or complete, along with metrics like screened papers or confirmed decisions.

You can open a stage directly from its card. The workflow is sequential, so later stages depend on the output of earlier ones, especially when full-text screening and extraction rely on the set of papers included upstream.

## Final report

When the review reaches the final stage, the system generates a narrative report that synthesizes findings from the included papers. The report is designed to give you a working draft that you can review, refine, and export.

It includes:

- Structured sections with headings and inline citations
- A PRISMA flow diagram with counts from each stage
- Export options for sharing or publication
