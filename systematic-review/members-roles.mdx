Three things control who does what in a systematic review:

|             | What it decides                                    |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Members** | Who is on the systematic review                    |
| **Access**  | What a member is allowed to do                     |
| **Roles**   | Which stages a member is responsible for verifying |

Set them in that order. Add the member, give them the right access, then assign them to the stages they will verify.

---

## Members

Members are the people who can open your systematic review. You can add anyone, inside or outside your organization, and remove them at any time.

Adding and removing members is limited to the **review creator**. Inside an organization workspace, **workspace admins** and **workspace owners** can do this as well.

### Adding a member

1. Open your systematic review and go to the **Members** tab.
2. Add the email address and choose **Full Access**, **Review**, or **View**.
3. Send the invitation
4. The person receives an email and must accept it before they can open the systematic review.

You can also generate a **view-only share link** for people who only need to watch progress. Anyone who redeems the link joins as a guest with View access and appears in the member list.

### Removing a member

| The member is                    | To remove them                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Invited by email                 | Revoke their access from member management. Access ends immediately.                                        |
| Joined by share link             | Revoke them individually from the member list. Revoking the link itself only stops new people from joining. |
| An organization workspace member | Remove them from the workspace to end access entirely.                                                      |

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  If the person you remove was named on a stage, open **Settings & Roles** and give that stage to someone else, or set an AI Review verifier back to **Anyone**, so the stage is not left waiting on someone who has left.
</Callout>

### Organization workspace members

If your systematic review sits inside an organization workspace, everyone in that workspace already has access to it. You do not need to invite them.

Their access is set automatically from their workspace role:

| Workspace role   | Access in the systematic review     |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Workspace admin  | Full Access, and can manage members |
| Workspace owner  | Full Access, and can manage members |
| Workspace editor | Review                              |
| Workspace viewer | View                                |

This is the fastest way to bring in internal team members, because access follows the workspace they already belong to.

**Change a workspace member's access for one review.** In the **Members** tab, pick a different access level next to their name. It applies to this review only, and a **Custom** label shows next to their name. To undo it, open the menu next to their access and select **Reset to workspace default**. Workspace owners and admins always keep Full Access.

## Access

Access determines what a member can do across the systematic review. There are three levels: **Full Access**, **Review**, and **View**.

|                            | Full Access | Review          | View |
| -------------------------- | ----------- | --------------- | ---- |
| View all stages            | Yes         | Yes             | Yes  |
| Add papers and PDFs        | Yes         | Yes             | No   |
| Remove papers and resolve duplicates | Yes | No            | No   |
| Initiate AI tasks and runs | Yes         | No              | No   |
| Change screening decisions | Yes         | With assignment | No   |
| Confirm AI decisions       | With assignment | With assignment | No |
| Vote or resolve conflicts in a Dual Review | With assignment | With assignment | No |
| Edit protocol              | Yes         | No              | No   |
| Edit settings              | Yes         | No              | No   |
| Manage access              | No          | No              | No   |
| Manage members             | No          | No              | No   |
| Export                     | Yes         | Yes             | No   |

"With assignment" means the member holds a role on that stage. In an AI Review, that is the verifier role: the stage's verifier is either that member or **Anyone**, the default. In a Dual Review, it is a Reviewer or Conflict Resolver role, and it applies to Full Access members too: nobody votes or resolves on a stage without being assigned to it.

### Full Access

Full Access runs the systematic review end to end. These members add and remove papers, resolve duplicates, start AI runs at every stage, edit the protocol and settings, change decisions at any stage, and export.

In an AI Review, Full Access members can override decisions **without being assigned to a stage**. Confirming a stage is different: it needs the assignment. If a stage's verifier is **Anyone**, any Full Access member can confirm it. If it is assigned to someone else, only that person can confirm it.

In a Dual Review, screening decisions belong to the assigned reviewers and resolver only. A Full Access member who holds no role on a stage can read the papers and comment, but cannot vote or resolve conflicts there. This keeps the two-reviewer record clean: every decision was made by one of the named people.

### Review

Review access is for people who take part in the review work without administrative control. They can view every stage, add papers and PDFs, and export. They cannot remove papers, resolve duplicates, start AI runs, edit the protocol, or change settings.

Members with Review access can change and confirm screening and extraction decisions **only on the stages they are assigned to**. That is what "with assignment" means in the table. Without an assignment, they can see the decisions but cannot act on them.

### View

View is read-only. These members see every stage and its current state but cannot add papers, make or confirm decisions, edit anything, or export. Use View for advisors, stakeholders, and collaborators who need visibility but should not change the review.

### Managing members is separate from access

No access level lets a member add people, remove people, or change anyone's access. That is why both **Manage access** and **Manage members** are No in every column, including Full Access.

Those permissions depend on who the person is, not what access they hold:

- The **review creator** can always manage members and access.
- Inside an organization workspace, **workspace owners** and **workspace admins** can as well.

This means you can give someone complete working control of the systematic review without handing over control of who else is on it.

## Roles

A role is a stage responsibility. Roles are what turn Review access into decision-making authority. Access says what a member is capable of; the role says which stages they are responsible for.

Which roles exist depends on the review method you chose when you created the review.

### Roles in an AI Review

Paperguide AI produces a decision at each stage, and an assigned member verifies it before the systematic review moves forward. The assigned member is the **verifier** for that stage.

| Verifier role                           | Responsibility                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Title & Abstract Screening verifier** | Reviews AI screening decisions on titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria, overrides where needed, and confirms the stage. |
| **Full-Text Screening verifier**        | Reviews AI screening decisions on full-text PDFs against the eligibility criteria, overrides where needed, and confirms the stage.       |
| **Data Extraction verifier**            | Checks AI-extracted values against the extraction fields, edits where needed, and confirms the extraction.                               |

Each stage is assigned separately. One person can verify every stage, or you can split them across the team.

### Roles in a Dual Review

Each screening stage has three roles instead of one verifier. Data extraction keeps its single verifier.

| Role                  | Responsibility                                                                                   |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Reviewer 1**        | Screens every paper in the stage independently                                                   |
| **Reviewer 2**        | Screens every paper in the stage independently                                                   |
| **Conflict Resolver** | Decides the papers the two reviewers disagreed on. Their decision is final.                      |
| **Data Extraction verifier** | Checks AI-extracted values against the extraction fields, edits where needed, and confirms. |

Rules for the screening roles:

- Every role goes to a named member. A dual review has no "anyone on the team" option.
- Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 must be different people.
- The conflict resolver may also be one of the reviewers.
- Both reviewers must be assigned before screening on that stage can start. The resolver can be added later.
- The people can differ between title & abstract screening and full text screening.

[How Dual Review works](/systematic-review/dual-review/how-it-works)

### Who can verify a stage in an AI Review

Every verifier starts as **Anyone** in a new review. Assigning a stage to a member narrows who is responsible for it.

| The verifier is            | Who verifies it                                             |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A named member**         | That member. Verification is their responsibility.          |
| **Anyone** (the default)   | Any member with Full Access or Review access can verify it. |

Assign a stage when you want one named person accountable for it, which is what most reviews need. Leave it on Anyone when anyone on the team should be able to pick up the work, which is useful for smaller reviews or when you want to share the load.

Either way, a View member can never verify a stage.

### Assigning a verifier

1. Set the member's access first. Only members with Review or Full Access can be assigned. A View member is not eligible.
2. Open **Settings & Roles**. Stage assignments are managed there, not in the Members tab.
3. Choose the verifier for each stage: **Title & Abstract Screening Verifier**, **Full-text Screening Verifier**, and **Extraction Verifier**. The member you choose receives an email letting them know.

Set the verifier for a stage before that stage starts. Verification is what advances the systematic review.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  The assigned verifier receives an email to verify once the AI screening or extraction is complete.
</Callout>

### What a verifier does

When the AI run for a stage completes, the assigned verifier works through the results:

1. Review each decision along with the evidence Paperguide AI cited for every criterion.
2. Override any decision they disagree with. For an exclusion, they select which criterion the paper failed.
3. Confirm the stage when the review is complete. This closes the stage and unlocks the next one.

Their decision is saved over the AI decision, and both are kept so the systematic review has a complete record of what the AI decided and what a human changed.

### Changing an assignment

Reassign a stage at any time from the review's settings. Two things to watch:

- Moving a member to **View** returns the stages they verified to **Anyone**. In a Dual Review, their reviewer and resolver roles become unassigned.
- Removing a member does not reassign their stages. Choose a new verifier, or set the stage back to **Anyone**.

### Replacing a reviewer in a Dual Review

Giving a reviewer role to a new person after the previous holder has already screened papers asks one question: **How should the new reviewer start?**

| Choice                              | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Continue from previous progress** | The previous reviewer's decisions stay, labelled with their name. The new reviewer can confirm each one as their own or change it. Nothing else changes.                                                                             |
| **Reset & start fresh**             | The previous reviewer's decisions are removed. The other reviewer's decisions stay. Affected papers lose their final decision and anything done for them downstream, and any generated report is deleted. The dialog shows the counts before you confirm. |

Changing the conflict resolver, or filling an empty role, asks nothing. Continue is the safer choice: the record stays complete and no work is lost.
