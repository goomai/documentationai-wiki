## Overview

Literature Review finds papers on your question, screens them against your requirements, and writes a cited report. You also get a table that compares the included papers, which you can extend and keep.

Use it for a focused question, such as a thesis chapter, the related work section of a paper, or the background for a grant application.

<Callout kind="info">

Literature Review works best on focused questions. For a very broad topic, such as "everything about climate change", narrow the question first.

</Callout>

## Start a literature review

<Steps>

<Step title="Open Literature Review" icon="play-circle">

On your Dashboard or in **New Chat**, select the **Literature Review** tab. You can also open **More tools** → **Literature Review** in the sidebar.

</Step>

<Step title="Describe your topic and requirements" icon="edit">

Type your research question and any requirements in the box. For example:

- "How does daily social media use relate to anxiety, depression, or self-esteem in young adults?"
- "Does personalized learning with AI tools improve student outcomes compared to traditional instruction?"

Add requirements in the same box so the review screens papers the way you want, such as the field, study types, years, or journal quality. For example: "2015 to present, RCTs in adults, Q1 or Q2 journals."

</Step>

<Step title="Choose your sources" icon="book">

Click the sources button below the box to choose where the review looks. All sources are on by default:

- **Research papers**: 200M+ public research papers
- **PubMed**: 39M+ biomedical and life-science papers
- **arXiv**: research preprints
- **Reference manager**: papers you've saved

With **Reference manager** on, click **Choose papers or folders** to review only specific papers from your library. When you search research papers, click **Filters** to narrow by year, citations, journal quality, language, paper type, or field of study.

</Step>

<Step title="Pick the review scope" icon="sliders-horizontal">

Send your question. When the review searches the research databases, a **Select review scope** dialog opens:

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Standard** | Screens about 100 papers and includes up to the top 20 in your review table and report. |
| **Extended** | Screens about 200 papers and includes up to the top 50, for a broader literature review. |

Click **Generate review**. Extended is a paid option: on the free plan, you see an upgrade button instead of **Generate review**.

If you review only papers from your reference manager, the review starts straight away.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What you get

The review runs as a chat, and you can follow each step as it searches, screens, and writes. When it finishes, open each result from its card in the chat:

- **Literature Report**: a narrative review with in-text citations. Hover a citation to see the paper and the supporting quotes.
- **Literature Review Table**: one row per included paper, with columns for key details such as methods and findings. Click a cell to see the evidence it came from.
- **Screening Decisions and Outcome Summary**: which papers were included or excluded, and why. Click **View screening criteria** to see the criteria used.

You can keep asking follow-up questions in the same chat, for example to compare studies or rewrite a section.

### Use the report

From the report, you can:

- **Copy with citations** to paste the report into another document.
- **Export** it as PDF or Word.
- **Save & edit** to save it to your Notebooks and keep editing it in AI Writer.

### Add a custom column

1. Click **Manage Columns**, then **Create custom column**.
2. Enter a **Column Name**, such as "Sample size" or "Age group".
3. Add instructions that describe what to extract from each paper, such as "Categorize participants as child, adult, or elderly."
4. Click **Create column**. Paperguide fills it in for every paper.

If a paper doesn't contain the information, the cell says so. To reuse a column in other tables, open its menu in **Manage Columns** and click **Save column**. It then appears under **Saved Columns**.

### Manage papers in the table

Select papers to **Add to My References**, **Remove selected**, or **Delete** them from the table.

<Callout kind="tip">

You can save the table only after it's filled. While the agent is still working, the button shows **Filling table…**. After saving, click **Open Saved Table** to open it in a new tab.

</Callout>

## Tips for better reviews

- Include the **population**, **intervention or exposure**, and **outcome** where you can. For example: "In university students, how does sleep duration relate to GPA and working memory?"
- Keep requirements short and specific. "2015 to present, RCTs in adults, Q1 or Q2 journals" works better than a long paragraph.
- Use **Choose papers or folders** when you already have a curated set of papers and want a review of just those.

## Find your reviews later

Each review is saved as a chat. Open **History** in the sidebar to return to it. Tables you saved are in **My Notebooks**.
