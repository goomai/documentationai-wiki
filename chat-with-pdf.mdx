## What is Chat with PDF?

Chat with PDF answers your questions from the full text of your PDFs, with citations that open the exact page. A PDF viewer sits beside the chat, so you can check the source while you ask.

Use it to:

- Get a summary of a long or dense paper
- Find the key claims, with supporting quotes
- Compare methods or results across several papers
- Find limitations, gaps, and open questions

## Start a chat

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Chat with PDF" icon="message-square" title-type="p">

  In the sidebar, open **More tools** → **Chat with PDF**.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your papers" icon="upload" title-type="p">

  - **Upload File**: choose PDFs from your device, or drag and drop them into the box.
  - **Select References**: pick papers from your [Reference Manager](/reference-manager). Only references with an attached PDF can be used.

  Then click the arrow to start the chat.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Wait for your files to be read" icon="loader" title-type="p">

  Each file shows its progress while Paperguide reads it. With one PDF, a summary is written for you as soon as the file is ready. With several, you get suggested questions to start from.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask your questions" icon="message-circle" title-type="p">

  Type a question, or pick a suggested one. Ask follow-ups such as "Where does it say that?" to get direct quotes.

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="info">

If you reach the upload limit, the **Upload File** button tells you. To chat with more papers, add them to your Reference Manager and choose them with **Select References**.

</Callout>

## Citations

Every answer includes in-text citations that point to the source passage.

- **Hover** a citation to see the cited sentences.
- **Click** a citation to open that page in the PDF viewer, with the sentence highlighted.

## Work with your files

Your files are listed in the **Sources** pane beside the chat. For each file, you can:

- **Mention**: point your next question at that file. You can also type **@** in the question box and pick a file.
- **My References**: save an uploaded file to your Reference Manager. It goes to the Unorganized folder.
- **Cite**: get a ready-to-copy citation for a reference.

To add more files to the same chat, click **Add File**.

When you chat with several papers, mention them by name to compare them, for example: "How does @Paper A differ from @Paper B in their experimental setup?"

## Chat with papers in your Reference Manager

You can also chat with papers straight from your library, without uploading them.

1. Open **Reference Manager**.
2. Select the papers you want, or open the folder that holds them.
3. Click **AI Chat** in the toolbar.

With papers selected, the chat covers those papers. With none selected, it covers the papers in the current view. If you select more than 100 papers, Paperguide asks you to confirm and includes the first 100.

## Share a chat

Click **Share** at the top of the chat, then **Create share link**. Anyone with the link can view a live, read-only copy of the chat, with no account needed. Click **Disable link** to stop access at any time.

## Tips for better results

- **Keep comparison sets focused.** For deep comparison or synthesis, results are usually better with around 10 papers in one chat.
- **Ask specific questions.** "What was the sample size and demographic breakdown?" works better than "Explain this paper."
- **Narrow down with follow-ups.** After a broad summary, ask "Show me only the limitations that relate to sample size."
- **Ask for evidence.** When you need quotes, ask for them directly, for example "What evidence supports the main conclusion?"
- **Start with the basics on hard papers.** Try "What are the most important concepts I need to understand before reading this paper?"
