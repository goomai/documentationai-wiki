## What is the Research Agent?

The Research Agent is Paperguide's all-in-one research assistant. It works across discovery, analysis, synthesis, and writing, so you don't need to switch tools for every question.

Unlike focused tools such as [AI Search](/ai-search), [Literature Review](/literature-review-agent), or [Chat with PDF](/chat-with-pdf), the Research Agent handles mixed, open-ended tasks that span several steps of your research.

## What it can help with

Use the Research Agent when you want one assistant to stay with you from early questions to drafted text. It can:

- Answer research questions with cited evidence
- Find relevant papers on any topic, or papers related to one you choose
- Compare papers, studies, and key findings
- Screen studies against your criteria and extract data into a table
- Find research gaps and study limitations
- Write a cited first draft, or add citations to your own draft
- Turn findings into diagrams, charts, and PRISMA flow diagrams

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Always review the agent's outputs, check cited papers directly, and apply your own expert judgment. Use the agent to support your research, not to replace critical appraisal or manual verification.
</Callout>

## Open the Research Agent

The Research Agent is the first tab on your Dashboard, and it's selected by default. You can also click **New Chat** in the sidebar, which opens it directly.

## Start with a task card

In **New Chat**, under **What do you want the agent to do?**, pick a task card to fill the box with a ready-made prompt. Replace the parts in \[brackets] with your own details, then send it. Filter the cards by **Discover**, **Analyze**, **Review**, **Write**, or **Visualize**.

| Category  | Tasks                                                                        |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Discover  | **Search papers**, **Research gap analysis**, **Find related papers**        |
| Analyze   | **Answer a question**, **Chat with papers**                                  |
| Review    | **Screen studies**, **Extract data**, **Literature review**                  |
| Write     | **Write a draft**, **Add citations**                                         |
| Visualize | **Generate a diagram**, **Chart study results**, **Create a PRISMA diagram** |

You can also type any request in your own words. For prompts covering everything the agent can do, see [Use cases and example prompts](/research-agent/use-cases).

## Add your own papers

- **Attach PDFs**: click **+** → **Add or upload files** to attach PDFs to your question. Paperguide reads them before it answers.
- **Mention library papers**: type **@** in the box to mention papers from your reference manager, or click **+** → **Add from reference manager**.

## Choose where it looks

Click the sources button below the box to choose where the agent looks for evidence. All sources are on by default:

- **Research papers**: 200M+ public research papers
- **PubMed**: 39M+ biomedical and life-science papers
- **arXiv**: research preprints
- **Reference manager**: papers you've saved

At least one source must stay on. With **Reference manager** on, click **Choose papers or folders** to limit the agent to specific papers. When you search research papers, click **Filters** to narrow by year, citations, journal quality, and more.

You can change the sources for each follow-up question in the chat.

## How to use the Research Agent

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe your goal and context" icon="edit" title-type="p">
    - State your **research question, project type, and constraints**, such as field, timeframe, or population.
    - Attach or mention any **key papers** you already have, and say where you are in the process: exploring, reviewing, or writing.

    The agent uses this context to tailor its searches, summaries, and suggestions.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Work across discovery, analysis, and writing" icon="refresh-ccw" title-type="p">
    - Ask it to **find and explain relevant papers**, flagging key methods, populations, and outcomes.
    - Request **comparisons, gap analysis, and limitations** based on the cited studies.
    - Move into **drafting or refining text**, such as introductions, related work sections, or discussion points.

    Keep these tasks in the same conversation so the agent keeps track of your work.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open and save what it creates" icon="folder" title-type="p">
    Tables, documents, diagrams, and other outputs open in a panel beside the chat. Save tables and documents to your Notebooks to keep working on them, or continue a document in [AI Writer](/ai-writer).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Research Gap Finder

For a deeper gap analysis, Research Gap Finder maps the evidence on a topic and finds graded research gaps worth studying next. It's available on paid plans. Open it from **More tools** → **Free Tools** → **Research Gap Finder**, or use the **Research gap analysis** task card in New Chat.

## When to use a dedicated tool

Use the Research Agent as your default when your work spans several stages, or when you're not sure which tool fits yet. Prefer a dedicated tool when:

- You need **targeted paper search** with filters, or a Boolean keyword search → use [AI Search](/ai-search).
- You want a **cited literature review** with a comparison table → use [Literature Review](/literature-review-agent).
- You want to **dig into specific PDFs** → use [Chat with PDF](/chat-with-pdf).
- You're mainly **writing or polishing a document** → use [AI Writer](/ai-writer).
