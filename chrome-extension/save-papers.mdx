## Overview

The Paperguide Chrome extension saves the paper or page you're reading straight to your Reference Manager. There's no need to download a PDF or type in citation details. It works on journals, publisher sites, PubMed, arXiv, Google Scholar, repositories, blogs and news sites.

## Save the page you're reading

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the paper or page" icon="globe" title-type="p">
    Go to the article, research paper or web page you want to save.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Add to My References" icon="plus" title-type="p">
    Click the Paperguide icon in your browser toolbar, then click **Add to My References**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose a folder" icon="folder" title-type="p">
    Pick the folder the reference should go in. It's saved to your current workspace.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What gets saved

- **The full-text PDF**, when the page has one.
- **The web page itself**, for sources that aren't PDFs, such as blog posts and news articles.
- **Citation details**, such as title, authors, journal, year and DOI.

Once a PDF is saved, Paperguide indexes it so you can chat with it and use it in other tools. See [Check that a PDF is ready for AI features](/chrome-extension/reference-manager#check-that-a-pdf-is-ready-for-ai-features).

## Save from buttons on research sites

On popular research databases and publisher sites, the extension adds Paperguide buttons to the page. Click one to save a paper, or add it to a folder, without opening the extension.
